Planigo

GPU-Accelerated B-spline Motion Planning
with Information-Geometric Optimization (IGO)

Planigo plans complete robot-arm motions through clutter by optimizing joint-space B-spline control points on the GPU.

Method

Motion planning in control-point space

The offline, full-horizon planner optimizes a complete trajectory from a fixed start to a selected inverse-kinematics (IK) goal.

01 Represent the motion

A cubic B-spline represents the joint-space motion. Six free controls, each containing seven joint values, define the search space while the endpoints remain fixed.

42 optimization variables

02 Optimize on the GPU

Mixture-model information-geometric optimization (MGIGO) samples candidate controls, scores them in parallel, and updates a Gaussian mixture for each free-control block.

03 Assess the output

A shared evaluator checks the output for goal accuracy, collisions, joint limits and dynamics at discrete trajectory samples. These checks determine benchmark success.

Circles: B-spline controls (filled: free; hollow: fixed). Squares: evaluation samples. Illustrations show joint-space curves, not Cartesian paths.

Evaluation protocol

Tasks. Endpoints pass shared pose, collision, map-boundary and joint-limit checks before optimization. Summary success rates and median planning times pool the retained task records across both datasets. Both planners are evaluated on those task IDs, including native planning failures. Success is measured on tasks with validated IK endpoints. Some MPiNets tasks are excluded because their IK candidates fail collision or clearance checks.

Timing. Synchronized host time; scene preparation, final evaluation, warmup, amortized initialization and file writes are excluded. Per-demo optimization time covers Planigo’s solver only. Full records: MB · MPiNets · per-task results.

Comparison. Runs dated 2026-10-18, with one seed42 run per planner and dataset. cuRobo uses 4 trajectory-optimization seeds within one native planning attempt, with particle/L-BFGS optimization and graph fallback disabled. Both methods use a shared final discrete evaluator; native IK, seeding and internal collision checks differ.
